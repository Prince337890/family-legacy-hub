IV.The first six months — do nothing brilliant

Windfalls are rarely lost to bad markets. They're lost to speed, pressure, and no plan.

    V.Choosing the family's financial advisor

    Interview two or three. Find candidates via NAPFA, Garrett Planning Network, or XY Planning Network — search "fee-only fiduciary." Ask each one:

    1. "Are you a fiduciary 100% of the time — in writing?" Anything but an instant yes is a no.
    2. "How exactly are you paid?" Fee-only (flat or % of assets). "Fee-based" is not the same — it can hide commissions.
    3. "Do you earn anything from products you recommend?" The right answer is no.
    4. "Who holds the money?" A major independent custodian, accounts in your name — never theirs.
    5. "Credentials, and experience with settlement windfalls?" Look for CFP®. Verify at brokercheck.finra.org and adviserinfo.sec.gov.
    6. "What do our first 90 days look like?" Good answers start with listening, taxes, and a written plan — not products.
    Walk away if you hear: guaranteed returns · "decide today" · an annuity or insurance product in the first meeting · requests to hold your money directly · vague fees.

    VI.The Haynes Family Charter

    Money divides families when the rules get invented in the moment, under pressure, one-on-one. We agree these before the money arrives — then the charter takes the heat, never a person.

    Our principles

    • This money is Beverly Haynes' legacy. Our goal: it is still visibly working for this family in ten years.
    • Each share is that person's alone — no explanations owed, no reaching into another's.
    • Big decisions happen slowly, together, in the open. Secrets are how money divides families.
    • We each enjoy some of it, guilt-free. Joy is in the plan on purpose.

    The loan & gift policy — word for word

    1. Any money request from outside the beneficiaries becomes a written request: amount, purpose, gift or loan. No verbal asks, no ambushes.
    2. Requests are reviewed only at the monthly meeting, never one-on-one. The only answer anyone gives in the moment: "The family reviews requests together at our monthly meeting."
    3. The default is a considered no, delivered by the group — so no one person is ever the villain.
    4. When we do help: a gift we can afford over a loan we'll resent. Unpaid loans end relationships; capped gifts don't.

    The monthly family money meeting

    Same time each month · one hour · rotating host.

    1. One minute for Grandma — what would Beverly think of what we're doing? (2 min)
    2. Where things stand — each person shares only what they choose to (10 min)
    3. Outside requests for money — reviewed together, per the policy (10 min)
    4. One thing we're learning (10 min)
    5. Shared goals — family fund, memorial giving, the annual trip (15 min)
    6. Decisions & who-does-what (10 min)

    The shared legacy (decide together)

    • A family fund — a fixed slice from each share (e.g., 5%) for emergencies, education, first-home boosts for the next generation.
    • A scholarship or annual gift in Beverly's name — ovarian cancer research, or a cause she loved.
    • An annual family trip paid from the fund's growth — her money actively bringing you together, on a schedule.
